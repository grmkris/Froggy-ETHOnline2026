# 9. Privy holds each person's Hedera key, as a cosmos-type wallet

Date: 2026-09-06

## Status

Accepted. Decided by the owner in the overnight planning of 6 Sep (see `docs/plan/DECISIONS.md`, "Custody moves to Privy").

## Context

Task 1.5 gave each person a Hedera account of their own, with the key generated by the server and sealed under `HEDERA_KEK`. Spike 1.8 asked whether Privy could hold that key instead. Privy's `raw_sign` refuses Ethereum and Solana wallets outright ("not supported for this low-level signature endpoint"), so the person's embedded wallet cannot be their Hedera account. It does sign a 32-byte hash for a cosmos-type wallet, whose key is secp256k1, the curve of a Hedera ECDSA account; the proof is in `docs/evidence/PRIVY.md`. Privy's policy engine cannot read raw bytes (a `signRawMessageBytes` rule must carry a condition and none applies), so the only rule that lets the signature through is `ALLOW *`.

## Decision

- Each person gets a cosmos-type Privy wallet created in their name (`owner: { user_id }`) under the policy `PRIVY_HEDERA_POLICY_ID`, with the agent key as an additional signer. Its public key names an EVM alias; the float pays that alias at the person's first top-up and Hedera creates the account. The record says which custody holds the key (`HederaCustody`), and a deployment without the policy id falls back to sealed keys.
- Hedera transactions are signed through `raw_sign` on the keccak256 hash of the transaction body, the way the Hiero SDK verifies an ECDSA signature. The hash is computed on our side with `@noble/hashes`, which is why `packages/wallet` may use it: Privy's `bytes` form of `raw_sign` refuses cosmos wallets, and the wallet package must not import the payments package or the Hedera SDK.
- The caps on the Hedera leg stay Froggy's, enforced by the mandate and the ledger before any signature is asked for. Privy's contribution is custody: no Hedera private key exists on Froggy's side, and `docs/` says so in those words.

## Consequences

- `HEDERA_KEK` and the sealed keys remain for deployments without the policy and for accounts opened before the switch; both custodies are read by the same code path.
- A person's Hedera account appears in their Privy account as a cosmos wallet. Privy's dashboard shows it; nothing in the app pretends it is an Ethereum wallet.
- The spike's ten purpose-made wallets and policies remain in the Privy app until archived.
